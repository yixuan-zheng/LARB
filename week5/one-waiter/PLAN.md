# Week 5 / A-lane: one-waiter cut — frozen plan

Written before any Week 5 inference (2026-10-29). Machine-readable source of truth: [plan.json](plan.json) (generated by [genplan.py](genplan.py), hashed in [source-lock.json](source-lock.json)). Deviations forced by the allocation are listed in "Scale" and were fixed before results existed.

## What this tests

Week 4 ended with a diagnosis, not a defect in delivery: with two slots, **one waiting request already means the machine is full**, yet the live rule waits for a second waiter. The candidate here is the smallest repair — the *same* rule, cue, caps and thresholds everywhere, with the wait threshold changed from 2 to 1 (`live_one`). Week 3/4 controller code is imported unchanged; [test_policy.py](test_policy.py) asserts its bytes against the Week 4 lock.

Four measured policies share one engine per job: `fixed2048`, `fixed4096`, `live` (unchanged Week 4 two-waiter), `live_one`. `full` exists only for the shared calibration and gate requests.

## Scale, and why

The design (24 questions × 4 policies × 3 workloads × 2 blocks = 576 answers) needs roughly 12
GPU-hours. Coverage is cut **by rank order, never by outcome**, in two sessions:

| Session | Allocation | Conditions run | Cohort slice |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 (2026-10-29 morning) | interactive H100, job 6663679, 3 h | gate, `constant_pressure` | rank positions 0–5 → 48 answers |
| 2 (2026-10-29 evening) | interactive H100, job 6665417, 4 h | gate, `one_at_a_time`, `slack_rush_recovery` | positions 6–11 and 12–17 → 48 + 48 answers |

Each condition takes the next **unspent** slice of the same published rank order
(`coverage.cohort_offset`). Session 2 does not reuse positions 0–5 because those questions already
have answers under all four policies: re-running them could only reproduce the same result, and the
premise verdict needs questions that no policy has answered yet. The slices were fixed before either
session-2 condition started, are still rank order, and were chosen without looking at any answer's
difficulty.

Two harness bounds were set per condition in session 2 **before** measurement, because they are
wall-clock limits, not measurements: `phase_seconds_by_condition = {one_at_a_time: 1800,
slack_rush_recovery: 1800}`. At ~60 tokens/s a single unconstrained request can spend 16,384
thinking tokens, which would have exceeded the original 900 s phase bound and aborted the whole
condition. Deadlines, arrivals, budgets and cues are untouched, so no outcome depends on this
number; it only converts a hard abort into a complete phase.

The session-2 gate therefore ran under plan revision 2 (six questions for `slack_rush_recovery`) and
the two measured conditions under revision 3 (nine, see `coverage.revision` in [plan.json](plan.json)).
No gate criterion reads a coverage count, and every run directory keeps the plan and lock it was
actually launched with, so the difference is visible rather than reconstructed.

Unfinished: `constant_pressure` on the session-2 questions, and any condition beyond six questions.
The claim that adapting wins when load *changes* rests on one six-question cohort.

Each condition is a separate process writing its own result directory; measured phases start in the frozen order and stop at a wall-clock cap recorded in the manifest. A truncated run reports exactly which phases completed.

## Mechanics gate (before any measured phase)

Scripted on already-exposed indices 1/4/5, so no cohort question is consumed: one long request runs alone, then two more arrive so that exactly one waits. The gate passes only if

1. every admitted request carries a scheduler admission decision recorded **before** it generates,
2. two requests are provably active in one scheduler step,
3. `live_one` takes a **live reduction mid-thinking** (with one waiter) and the forced cue completes to a normal final answer,
4. `fixed2048` forces a complete uncapped transition at exactly 2,048 tokens.

## Frozen analysis rules

- Primary: correct final answer, normal completion, received before the deadline on the client clock from scheduled arrival.
- Quality guard per workload: `live_one` may not finish with more than one fewer correct answer than `fixed2048` (both on-time and eventual).
- Also reported per workload: eventual correctness, median and slowest completion, queue wait, thinking tokens, total output tokens, cues spent, cuts that bound, cap failures, paired per-question changes.
- Premise verdict (one-at-a-time): extra thinking counts only if `fixed4096` beats `fixed2048` on the same question in **both** blocks.
- Fix verdict: `live_one` matches `fixed2048` on-time efficiency under pressure inside the quality guard.
- No reruns for an unfavorable result; failures, caps and partial coverage stay in the record.

## Files outside the source lock

`session2.sh`, `session2b.sh`, `watcher.sh`, `st.sh`, `monitor2.sh` and `verdicts.py` were written
after the lock was frozen. They are launcher, observer and reader files: the engine imports none of
them, and `verdicts.py` only reads already-scored rows. They are deliberately absent from
[source-lock.json](source-lock.json), which still describes exactly the software that produced the
answers. `launch.sh` was left frozen even though a second orphan-engine failure made its cleanup
insufficient; the fix went into `session2b.sh` instead, so the engine-facing files stayed byte-identical.
