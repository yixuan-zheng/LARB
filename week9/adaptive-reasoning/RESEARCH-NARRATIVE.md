# Research narrative and evidence map

**Question.** Can a model's choice of reasoning effort and a server's response to load jointly deliver more correct answers before deadlines? More reasoning uses decode capacity and can delay other requests. Cutting too aggressively can remove reasoning needed for correctness. The original target was at least 5% higher correct-on-time goodput with no more than two percentage points of eventual accuracy loss.

## Why explicit prediction was difficult

Difficulty describes the problem. Short-budget failure risk is the chance of getting it wrong under a particular solver, prompt and cap. Marginal benefit is the difference in correctness probability under a longer versus shorter policy. A difficult problem may defeat both policies. An easy-looking problem can still benefit from more thought on some draws. Admission predicts over full requests. Checkpoint prediction is conditional on still reasoning at that point and can ask a different question about the same prefix.

## Why train the mode decision

Outcome-based self-routing can optimize the decision without labeling a unique correct budget. We adapted [Learning When to Think](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.20256), which uses mode rewards, forced exploration and GRPO. Its training used 90 updates, 128 prompts per update, eight draws, four H100s and three training seeds. Its caps apply to response length during training and it evaluates uncapped. We used pinned Qwen3-4B, rank-16 q/v LoRA, a three-token action set, and enforced reasoning-only caps for Low/direct, Medium/2,048 and High/8,192 plus a distinct 2,048-token answer allowance. We reward verified correctness with mode-specific length discounting, center rewards within rollout groups, warm up with forced mode exploration, and mask injected bridge and closure tokens. The two screens used AdamW at 8e-6 with gradient clipping at 1. The reward was zero for a wrong answer. For correct Low, Medium and High answers it was 1.3 × 0.99984^T, 1.2 × 0.99994^T, and 1, respectively, with T the decoded token count. The mixed run used four questions per update and six rollouts per question, with six forced-exploration warm-up updates before free routing. Its later vLLM backend checked behavior likelihood parity against PyTorch. The training objective omitted shared queueing and deadline effects. [Single-question screen](screen/screen.py) · [Batched follow-up](backend/followup.py)

- The Omni-MATH screen ran 22 updates, one training question per update, one training seed and 33 held-out questions. Learned routing got 60.6% at 6,757 charged tokens versus 63.7% for a fixed-mode mixture at matched cost. It chose High 28 times and Low zero times.
- The full mixed follow-up exceeded the one-job throughput estimate. The 96-question fallback completed 24 updates and 576 rollouts. Its 96 held-out questions used one draw per mode. High selections increased from 6/32 on GSM8K to 12/32 on MATH and 20/32 on Omni-MATH. The checkpoint used for serving is step 24, job 7281434. The source pattern was encouraging, but the follow-up did not beat within-source shuffle or the same-checkpoint fixed mixture. The 96-question diagnostic in the [workload note](WORKLOAD-NOTE.md) quantifies the same marginal allocation problem the explicit predictors targeted. The earlier 56% hindsight token headroom on 33 questions depends on observing all outcomes. It does not estimate learned savings. Limited training scale, weak within-source signals and mismatch with the serving objective remain plausible, overlapping explanations.

## How serving changed the question

Combined pairs the learned action with pressure-aware admission and in-flight caps. Shuffle preserves each source's learned mode mix while breaking the question assignment, and tests the incremental value of learned placement. Its strength motivated a deployable global probabilistic policy with a development-only 40% High rate. The final replay measured that policy with and without load adaptation. The comparison shows usable engineering and a competitive cheap baseline on the observed trace. It does not validate learned question-specific routing.

See [final live results](FINAL-REPORT.md) and the [complete arm table](results/closeout/live.csv). On the eight-slot sustained condition, combined beats task-only by 8.8% in goodput with equal eventual accuracy and 18.9% fewer tokens, clearing the point-estimate success rule. Against load-only it improves goodput by 19.4% and uses 15.6% fewer tokens, but one fewer correct answer out of 48 is a 2.08-point loss, just beyond tolerance. It also beats fixed 4K and 8K on goodput and tokens while losing more than two accuracy points. It ties the stronger shuffle's goodput and trails in eventual accuracy and tokens. On the 24-request final trace, each coin-plus-load seed gains 25% goodput over combined but loses one or two eventual correct answers, beyond tolerance. Coin-only seed 151 clears both point criteria versus combined; seed 411 loses one eventual answer. One answer at 24 requests is 4.17 percentage points. These small samples cannot resolve a two-point noninferiority margin.

The agent interface fixtures established safe tool-call continuation at binding reasoning caps. Customer fidelity blocked full episodes. Whether turn state provides better effort signals remains untested. Further independent training is closed. The next work is integration and appropriately pressured serving scaling.

Combined couples a model-selected initial action with system-side load intervention. Related methods that use system-side difficulty estimation or confidence-driven termination solve different routing or termination subproblems. We did not directly benchmark LASER. The empirical contribution is the matched comparison of learned and prediction-free allocations under the same deadline-aware controller, including an ablation that exposed the value of a strong alternative control.
