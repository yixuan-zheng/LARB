# 3. How the closest work evaluates

This file began as a synthesis of the repository's own related-work audits: the full-paper review in
`week4/literature-audit/`, the method comparison in `week6/admission-timing-audit/RELATED-WORK.md`,
and the reading list in `week6/research-handoff/README.md`. It has since been checked against the
papers themselves (November 2026, versions in 3.7); 3.3 records what that check confirmed and
corrected. It is still not a reproduction of any competing system, and ACER is still abstract-only.

## 3.1 The methods and how they were measured

| Work | What it changes | Executed or simulated | Quality signal | Primary metrics | Scale of evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **LASER** (WASA 2026) | Prompt-length and load budgets; confidence exit moved by an EMA of *active* requests | RTX 4090 profiles, then FIFO **simulation** | Accuracy, reported apart from SLO | Mean/P95 latency, SLO attainment, QPS | Qwen3-4B and DS-R1-7B on one 4090; 4 benchmarks of 40-1,319 items; no code link |
| **PASCAL** (HPCA 2026) | Phase-aware priorities, answer pacing, migration at phase boundaries | Profile-validated 8-node **simulation** (E2E MAPE 1.6%, TTFT 12.6%) | None: token counts generated by another model | TTFT, QoE-based SLO violations | One 32B model; 300 requests per experiment; 2 chat prompt sets; no code link |
| **Certaindex / Dynasor** | Probes the CoT for answer stability; allocates budget; gang/SJF scheduling | **Executed** on A100 | Stability as a proxy, matched accuracy | Tokens-to-accuracy, P90 deadline attainment | 7/14/32B on AIME24, AMC23, MATH-500, plus SC/MCTS/Rebase; public code |
| **P-PAS+** | Max-batched-tokens switched by prefill/decode pressure: a scheduler knob, not reasoning depth | **Executed** on a vLLM fork | None: synthetic prompts, fixed 16-256-token outputs | End-to-end latency, TTFT, TPOT | 3B on one RTX 5090, 5 seeds; 27-30B 4-bit, 6 x 11 replayed runs, 33 GPU-h; public code |
| **UniBoost** (ICML 2026) | Priority boosting, KV-aware preemption hysteresis, online tail statistics | **Executed** on 8 x A100 | None (scheduling only) | Mean/P90/P99 TTFT and TTLT, throughput | 8B/34B/72B; 10k requests per workload; Azure, ShareGPT, s1k traces |
| **Queueing-aware allocation** | Static per-task budgets from an M/G/1 model with a known mixture | Qwen3-8B measurements, then **simulation** | Accuracy fitted per task and budget | Latency under a weighted-accuracy utility | 8B on A100; 6 task types x 250 instances x 3 runs |
| **ACER** (Princeton thesis) | Reasoning depth from each request's remaining latency budget | Unknown: login-restricted | Author-reported; "goodput" undefined publicly | Accuracy, tail latency, goodput, violations | 4 model-dataset configurations; abstract only |
| **DeepConf, Conformal Thinking, length-aware scheduling** | Confidence filtering of samples; risk-calibrated stopping; output-length distributions | Not audited here | Sample selection, error rate, length prediction | Accuracy and tokens, or latency | DeepConf needs many samples, Conformal a validation split, TIE per-prompt length samples; none measures a deadline |

Two patterns survive. Most published serving results are **simulations**; the systems that really run
(P-PAS+, UniBoost, Dynasor) either do not score answers at all or score them without a deadline.

The second pattern is about comparison sets, and it matters more than it looks. LASER and ACER
compare against fixed *confidence thresholds*; P-PAS against fixed *batch-token* budgets; UniBoost
against SJF/SRPT; Dynasor against uniform allocation and a length threshold. Across the six papers
whose evaluation sections are readable, none puts a single global token cap, tuned on the same
workload, next to an adaptive reasoning policy. That is exactly the competitor that ties or beats our
live policy ([02-evidence.md](02-evidence.md), 2.3). The literature does not contradict our result;
it has not run the experiment. It also means LASER's and ACER's gains are net of a probe cost that
both arms pay, while ours would be measured against a baseline that never probes.

## 3.2 The evaluation envelopes, next to ours

| Study | Model and hardware | Workload shape | Deadline / SLO | Correct *and* on time? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LASER | Qwen3-4B and DS-R1-7B, one 4090 | 3-14k-token CoT; Poisson 0.5-8 QPS; 1/5/1 bursts | Fixed d = 30 s | No: separate marginals |
| PASCAL | 32B, 8 simulated H100 nodes | Chat prompts, 300 requests | QoE from TPOT < 0.95 | No: no correctness |
| Dynasor | 7/14/32B, A100 | Math CoT and program workloads | SLO scale x trial-derived difficulty x base | No: separate marginals |
| P-PAS+ | 3B and 27-30B (4-bit), one RTX 5090 | 25k in / 32 out, then 8-128k / 16-256 | None: latency only | No |
| UniBoost | 8B/34B/72B, 8 x A100 | 10k requests, Azure/ShareGPT/s1k, 20-70% reasoning | SLO scale relative to P95 at 0.9 load | No |
| Queueing allocation | Qwen3-8B, A100 | Six task types, Poisson, known mixture | None: mean time inside the utility | Accuracy only, not per request |
| ACER | Unknown | Unknown | Two-tier SLO | Maybe, if "goodput" means it |
| **This rig** | Qwen3-4B, one H100, two slots | Short prompts, 4-8k thinking, 2k answer | 1.5x calibration (~236 s) | **Yes: correct-on-time** |

The shapes divide cleanly. LASER and Dynasor are decode-heavy, like us. P-PAS is prefill-heavy by two
orders of magnitude (25k in, 32 out), so its crossover is a prefill-interference effect our regime
cannot reproduce; the converse also holds, and UniBoost's decode-side scheduling effect is much
closer to our problem than the MBT result is.

The deadline column is the most useful one. LASER's 30 s deadline yields 12-18% SLO attainment under
burst on a 4090, and because that SLO counts wrong answers as served it upper-bounds a correct-on-time
rate; our 236 s deadline yielded 3-9 misses out of 40 per policy in weeks 5-6. Both are coherent, but
only the first sits in the 15-40% miss band that
[06-engineering-gaps.md](06-engineering-gaps.md), 6.8, argues keeps the metric informative. No study
in the table reports our intersection except possibly ACER's "goodput", whose definition is behind
the login. The joint metric is therefore defensible, provided it is defined so that it reduces to SLO
attainment when every answer is correct and to accuracy when deadlines never bind, and reported next
to both marginals.

## 3.3 What the check confirmed, and what it corrected

Three findings are worth recording. The budget component carries most of the published gain: LASER's
ablation credits it with a 23.2% latency reduction and credits the adaptive threshold with 1.4
accuracy points at 4.7% more latency, and P-PAS+ beats the best static budget by only 3.0-3.1%. That
is the published form of our unwelcome fixed-short finding. Probing is not free, and the authors say
so: Dynasor reports that probing every step forces its samples into sequential batches, raising mean
latency from 289 s to 366 s (26.6%) for about 3.4% more token savings, which is why its end-to-end
runs use a fixed probe step. Third, the joint metric is less rare than our earlier audits implied: ACER
may report it as goodput, and most of the other systems omit it only because they never score an
answer.

| Earlier statement | What the source says | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| P-PAS+: two 27-30B models, ~500-1,000 requests per condition, ten seeds | The primary study is Qwen2.5-3B on one RTX 5090, 5 seeds, 25k-token prompts; the 27-30B 4-bit runs are the extension, 6 conditions x 11 replayed seeds, 33 GPU-h | Scheduler evidence only; no quality signal |
| Dynasor: single-chain CoT on GSM8K/MATH | CoT is on AIME24, AMC23 and MATH-500; GSM8K belongs to the self-consistency workloads; probe intervals are 32-320 tokens | 11-29% is the CoT saving; 50% and 3.3x are best cases |
| PASCAL: quality is not the main target | There is no quality signal; token counts come from o4-mini | Cite it for phase scheduling only |
| All papers that report uncertainty group by question | The norm is repeat runs over a frozen benchmark, with matched traces in P-PAS; question-level clustering is rare because correctness is rarely scored | Keep our pairing as an improvement, not as a norm we match |

What is still unknown is ACER's algorithm, figures, cost accounting and goodput definition. The item
page lists a 2.7 MB PDF, but the download redirects to a login. Its abstract reports +10.5 pp accuracy
at tight SLOs, -38% tail latency at matched accuracy, +21% goodput and SLO violations falling from
28% to under 1% in a two-tier experiment. It is the closest work to our metric and the only one we
cannot check.

## 3.4 Datasets, sizes, and the resolution they buy

The audits recorded which benchmarks appear but not how large the slices are. The papers are
explicit, and the sizes set the resolution bar.

| Pool | Items | Where it appears | What that size can resolve |
|---|---:|---|---|
| GSM8K | 1,319 | LASER, Dynasor (SC), queueing allocation | Saturated for a 4B model; filler, not signal |
| MATH-500 | 500 | LASER, Dynasor | +/-4 points marginal; we use 100 items or 21-question slices |
| AMC 2023 | 40 | LASER, Dynasor | +/-16 points; a hard tail, not a comparison set |
| AIME 2024 / 2025 | 30 each | Dynasor, DeepConf | +/-18 points; only paired, same-item analysis helps |
| GPQA-Diamond | 198 | LASER; queueing allocation (GPQA); Conformal pools it with AIME | +/-7 points; one-letter answers make the answer phase free |
| BBH, ARC, CRUXEval | Not stated per split | Queueing allocation | Mixture breadth, not depth |
| Azure, ShareGPT, LMSYS, Alpaca | 10k requests or 1M conversations | UniBoost, TIE | Scheduling only; no correctness |

At 50% accuracy a marginal 95% interval is about +/-4 points on 500 independent items, +/-10 on 100
and +/-28 on 12. Published one-point accuracy claims are not marginal: they compare the same items
under two policies, which is how LASER can claim a one-point cost on 500 MATH-500 problems. Published
unpaired effects are large instead: 11-50% in tokens, up to 38% in latency, 3-10 points in accuracy
or SLO attainment. Our own effects (2-12.5 points on-time, 15-17% tokens, 27-48% latency) sit inside
that range; our samples are ten to forty times smaller. The plan's 500-2,000 responses per arm is not
ambition, it is the entry price for the claims these papers make.

What the papers still do not give us is the curve we need most: none reports Qwen3-4B accuracy
against fixed 4k/6k/8k budgets on a hard pool. LASER's Qwen3-4B numbers use confidence exit,
Dynasor's curves are for DeepSeek-distilled models, and both use full pools. That curve remains our
own measurement ([04-datasets.md](04-datasets.md), 4.5).

Arrivals are Poisson in LASER, PASCAL, Dynasor, P-PAS and the queueing study. UniBoost is the
exception: it replays the public Azure trace with scaled timestamps, the only realistically bursty
source in the set. Nobody publishes a trace of a reasoning service, so a replayed chat or coding
trace contributes arrival shape and nothing else. Our scripted burst is a stronger stress than
Poisson and cheaper than a trace; the Azure trace is the defensible exogenous alternative if the next
rig wants one ([05-ideas.md](05-ideas.md), idea 10).

## 3.5 Methodology lessons worth copying

1. **Always include an intermediate static budget.** LASER's ablation and P-PAS+'s 3% margin both say
   the static setting carries most of the gain (3.3). Sample the middle of the grid (5k, 6k), not only
   the endpoints.
2. **Charge the whole mechanism.** Dynasor's own probe experiment costs 26.6% latency for 3.4% extra
   savings. Our cut reuses the prefix and forces the cue, which looks free; measure the cue, the
   snapshots and the row bookkeeping, not just the tokens saved.
3. **Report the trade-off, not one number.** UniBoost improves P99 by 35% while worsening mean
   latency by 19%; P-PAS+ improves mean latency by 3% while 91% of requests get faster.
4. **Declare the deadline and check its regime.** Dynasor builds difficulty-dependent deadlines from
   more than 100 trial runs per query; LASER fixes 30 s and lands at 12-18% attainment; ours lands at
   78-93% on-time. A recipe can manufacture or erase a goodput effect, so report the realised misses.
5. **Separate reversible from irreversible actions.** Preemption can resume; a forced answer cannot
   recover the reasoning it skipped. Priority-boosting results do not transfer to cuts.
6. **Check the realised queue and the workload shape.** P-PAS fires on competing prefills at 25k
   input tokens; ours fires on a waiting request in a decode-dominated workload. Same word, different
   physics. Snapshot evidence, not the nominal label.
7. **Report curves, not points.** Dynasor's token-to-accuracy curves, LASER's per-budget accuracies
   and P-PAS's static sweep all buy resolution without more questions. A frontier is also the only
   honest way to compare adaptive and non-adaptive spending.
8. **Do not inherit the field's weakest claim.** "No accuracy drop" on 30-item AIME sets is common
   here, usually with repeat runs rather than a paired design, and is exactly the claim our last six
   weeks have shown cannot be supported at that size. Copy their designs, not their confidence.

## 3.6 What that implies for the next rig

- **Keep fixed 4k/6k/8k arms and say why.** No readable paper here puts a tuned global token cap
  beside an adaptive policy. Our data says that arm is the real competitor, so running it is not a
  control, it is a contribution.
- **Decide the probe arm explicitly.** LASER and ACER compare against fixed confidence thresholds,
  which pay the same probe cost. Our non-probing cut is a different claim: cheaper, but blind to
  whether the reasoning was near finishing. Add one coarse probe arm (Dynasor probes every 32-320
  tokens) and charge its extra answer tokens, or scope confidence-based exit out in writing.
- **Define the primary metric once, and report its two marginals.** State its degenerate cases, so it
  reduces to accuracy without deadlines and to SLO attainment with perfect answers. That also makes a
  future comparison with ACER's "goodput" possible.
- **Set the deadline in the informative band.** Target 15-40% misses for the reference policy, as 6.8
  says. LASER's 30 s regime is the external precedent; weeks 5-6 sat at the saturated end.
- **Buy resolution deliberately.** Either 500 independent items from a pool whose slope we have
  measured, analysed as paired within-question differences, or curves over budgets on a smaller pool,
  or both. A 12-question trace can illustrate a mechanism but cannot carry the 3-point clause.
- **Keep a scheduler control in view.** UniBoost moves P99 by 35% without touching reasoning, and
  P-PAS moves latency by 3% with a scheduler knob. If a stronger scheduler empties the queue, the cut
  has nothing left to do; that is a finding, not a nuisance.
- **Stay decode-heavy, and bridge where the model and pool overlap.** LASER, Dynasor and UniBoost are
  decode-bound, so their evidence transfers better than P-PAS's prefill result. LASER also runs
  Qwen3-4B on MATH-500, which makes accuracy and compression a cheap external anchor for our grader,
  even though its latency is simulated.
